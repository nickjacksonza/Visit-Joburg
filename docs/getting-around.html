Transport and safety

Getting Around Johannesburg

Johannesburg is large and spread out. Most visitors use the Gautrain for longer trips and ride-hailing apps for everything else.

From the Airport

O.R. Tambo International Airport is about 25 km east of the city centre. The Gautrain airport line takes you to Sandton in about 15 minutes. Change at Sandton for Rosebank, Park Station and Pretoria. Ride-hailing cars and metered taxis wait outside the terminals.

Gautrain

The Gautrain is a fast, clean rapid rail line that links the airport, Sandton, Rosebank, the city centre and Pretoria. Pay with a Gautrain card or a contactless bank card at the gates. Feeder buses run from most stations.

Ride-Hailing and Taxis

Uber and Bolt both work well in Johannesburg and are the easiest way to travel door to door, especially at night. Check the number plate and the driver's name before you get in.

Buses and Minibus Taxis

Rea Vaya is a bus rapid transit system with its own lanes, linking Soweto with the city centre. Minibus taxis carry most of the city's commuters. They are cheap but hard to use without local help, so ask a guide or your host first.

Driving

South Africa drives on the left. A car is useful for day trips, such as the Cradle of Humankind. In the city, expect heavy traffic at rush hour and power cuts that can turn off traffic lights. Treat a dark traffic light as a four-way stop.

Staying Safe

  • Keep your phone, camera and bag out of sight on the street and in the car.
  • Use ride-hailing instead of walking after dark, even for short trips.
  • Go with a local guide in the city centre and in Soweto.
  • Keep car doors locked and windows up at traffic lights.
  • In an emergency, call 10111 for the police or 112 from a mobile phone.
